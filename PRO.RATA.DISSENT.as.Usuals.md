This cognitive swerving allows individuals to reclaim their internal moral and spiritual autonomy through alternate neural models like meditation, prayer, or systemic non-compliance.1. Instead, they are experienced as "stains"—accumulated marks of physical or mental exhaustion, stress, and systemic wear.Aperture Gates: These are the perceptual and societal filters that restrict human experience. When these gates narrow, they force individuals to constantly "count time" and anticipate upcoming moral requirements, killing spontaneous human joy and natural regularity.2. The Prefrontal Negotiation: Swaying the Natural ModelFaced with the exhausting constraints of linear time and labor dominance, the brain does not simply submit. It actively negotiates for psychological survival.Prefrontal Adaptation: The prefrontal cortex acts as the executive negotiator. It constantly tries to balance the harsh external reality (the linear, causal model) with internal human needs.Swaying the System: The brain alters its internal perception of time to "sway" or bend the rigid rules of the external environment. This prevents total neural burnout.3.
